Exploring Earth's Physical Features

Physical geography examines natural processes and features of the Earth's surface, atmosphere, and hydrosphere.

Overview

Earth's surface is shaped by forces from inside the planet that build land and by forces on the surface that wear it down.

Key Concepts
  • Plate tectonics: the crust is split into slowly moving plates
  • Weathering and erosion: breaking down and carrying away rock
  • Landforms: mountains, valleys, plains, plateaus and coasts
  • Volcanoes and earthquakes: energy released at plate boundaries
Quick Facts
A few cm/year typical plate movement8,849 m height of Mount EverestRing of Fire holds about three-quarters of the world's volcanoes
Detailed Notes

Mountains form when plates collide and push rock upward, while rifts and ocean trenches form where plates pull apart or one slides under another. Rivers, ice, wind and waves then carve these landforms over millions of years.

Volcanoes appear where molten rock reaches the surface. Mount Etna in Sicily lies where the African and Eurasian plates meet. Explore the mountain and volcano models below.

Major Subtopics:
  • Geomorphology: Landforms and their formation processes
  • Climatology: Weather patterns and climate systems
  • Hydrology: Water distribution and cycles
  • Biogeography: Species distribution and ecosystems
  • Pedology: Soil formation and classification

Human Impact and Settlement Patterns

Human geography studies the relationship between people and their environments, focusing on cultural, political, and economic aspects.

Overview

Where people live depends on water, food, trade routes and safety, and people in turn reshape the land.

Key Concepts
  • Population density: people per square kilometre
  • Urbanisation: growth of towns and cities
  • Settlement patterns: nucleated (clustered), linear (along roads or rivers), dispersed
  • Migration: people moving between places
Quick Facts
~8 billion people on EarthOver half of people live in citiesRivers hosted many of the first cities
Detailed Notes

Early settlements grew near fresh water and fertile soil. Today, cities grow around trade, jobs and services, which draws people from rural areas.

Growth brings pressures such as traffic, housing shortages, pollution and loss of farmland, so planners study patterns to design better cities.

  • Economic Geography: Resource distribution and trade
  • Political Geography: Borders, territories, and governance

Sustainability and Resource Management

Environmental geography examines the complex relationships between human societies and their natural surroundings, focusing on sustainability.

Overview

Sustainability means using resources so that future generations can meet their needs too.

Key Concepts
  • Renewable resources: replaced naturally, like sun, wind and water
  • Non-renewable resources: finite, like coal, oil and gas
  • Conservation: protecting and using resources wisely
  • Carbon footprint: greenhouse gases caused by an activity or person
Quick Facts
1987 Brundtland report defined sustainable development17 UN Sustainable Development Goals2015 goals adopted by all UN members
Detailed Notes

Fossil fuels took millions of years to form and release carbon dioxide when burned. Solar, wind, hydro and geothermal energy produce far fewer emissions.

Individuals and governments can cut waste by reducing, reusing and recycling, protecting forests and wetlands, and managing water carefully.

  • Natural Resources: Distribution and management
  • Environmental Hazards: Disasters and risk assessment
  • Conservation: Biodiversity and protected areas
  • Climate Change: Impacts and adaptation strategies
  • Sustainable Development: Balancing growth and conservation

Modern Geographic Analysis

Geographic techniques include various tools and methods used to collect, analyze, and present geographic data.

Overview

Modern geography uses satellites and computers to collect, map and analyse information about places.

Key Concepts
  • GIS: software that layers map data
  • Remote sensing: collecting data from satellites and aircraft
  • GPS: satellite positioning
  • Map scale: the ratio of map distance to real distance
Quick Facts
4+ satellites needed for a GPS position in 3D1:50,000 means 1 cm on the map is 500 m on the groundLayers let GIS combine roads, rivers and population
Detailed Notes

A GIS stacks layers, such as rivers, roads, land use and population, so patterns become visible. This helps with flood planning, choosing sites for schools, and tracking deforestation.

Satellite images show change over time, allowing scientists to compare the same place across years.

  • Spatial Analysis: Pattern recognition and modeling

Study of World Regions

Regional geography focuses on the unique characteristics of specific areas, analyzing both physical and human features.

Overview

Regional geography groups places by shared physical and human features such as climate, landscape, language and culture.

Key Concepts
  • Continents: seven large landmasses
  • Climate regions: areas with similar temperature and rainfall
  • Cultural regions: shared language, religion or traditions
  • Interdependence: regions linked by trade and travel
Quick Facts
7 continents~71% of Earth's surface is oceanAsia is the largest continent by area and population
Detailed Notes

The seven continents are Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia/Oceania. Regions can also be defined by climate, such as tropical, desert, temperate and polar.

Comparing regions helps explain why some places are densely populated while others are almost empty.

  • Cultural Regions: Language families and religious zones
  • Economic Regions: Trade blocs and development zones
  • Political Regions: Countries and territorial divisions
  • Biogeographic Regions: Ecosystems and biodiversity hotspots

Atmospheric Science and Climate Patterns

The study of weather patterns, climate zones, and atmospheric phenomena that shape our planet's environments.

Overview

The atmosphere is a layer of gases that protects life, shapes weather and keeps Earth warm.

Key Concepts
  • Weather vs climate: weather is short-term; climate is the long-term average
  • Atmospheric layers: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere
  • Greenhouse effect: gases trapping heat
  • Water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation
Quick Facts
78% / 21% nitrogen / oxygen in air~12 km average top of the troposphere30 years typical period used to describe climate
Detailed Notes

Most weather happens in the troposphere, the lowest layer. Warm air rises, cools and forms clouds, while differences in air pressure create wind.

The greenhouse effect is natural and keeps the planet warm enough for life, but adding more greenhouse gases strengthens it and warms the climate.

  • Climate Change Evidence: Past, present, and future trends
  • Weather Forecasting: Tools and prediction methods
